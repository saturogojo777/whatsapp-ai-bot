# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want contributing to HueForge to be a genuinely good experience — for first-time
contributors, for designers who don't write much JavaScript, and for people whose
first language is not English. We pledge to make participation harassment-free for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic
status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that builds this community:

- Being kind to people who ask "obvious" questions. They are not obvious to everyone.
- Critiquing the code, the colour maths, or the design — never the person.
- Assuming good faith, then asking a clarifying question.
- Accepting that "no" is a complete answer to a feature request, and that a fork is a
  legitimate way to disagree.
- Crediting the person whose issue taught you something.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- Harassment, insulting or derogatory comments, personal or political attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issues or pull requests.
- Dismissing accessibility concerns as "nitpicking". In this project they are never
  nitpicks.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages — and when representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report incidents to the maintainers by opening a confidential report through the
repository's **Security → Report a vulnerability** flow, or by contacting a maintainer
directly. Reports are handled privately, and the reporter's identity is not shared
without consent.

Maintainers will respond within seven days and may take any action they consider
appropriate, from a private warning to a permanent ban. Decisions are documented
internally so the same situation is handled consistently.
